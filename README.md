# closebot vs salesape: which AI appointment setter fits your CRM, budget and setup patience

Searching for "closebot vs salesape" usually means you have already decided you want an AI agent answering inbound leads. What you have not decided is whether you want a tool you build yourself on a cheap, published plan, or a managed service where someone else trains the agent and you wait a few weeks for it to go live.

Those are genuinely different purchases, and the pricing pages tell the story before any feature list does. One publishes every tier, including a free plan. The other publishes nothing and asks you to book a call. That single difference shapes almost every other comparison you will read below.

## The short answer

If you run a marketing agency or you live inside GoHighLevel or HubSpot and want an agent you can build, test and launch yourself this week, CloseBot is the better fit. You can start on a free plan and see how the conversation engine behaves before you pay anything.

If your inbound leads are going cold because nobody replies fast enough, you have no interest in building or maintaining an agent, and a $1,000-plus monthly budget is not a shock, SalesAPE's done-for-you model is the more honest match.

Note the shape of that split. It is not primarily about which AI is smarter. It is about who owns the work.

## What each product actually is

CloseBot is a conversational AI platform where you build agents that qualify leads, handle objections, follow up and book appointments on your calendar. It is agentic rather than scripted: you define an objective, attach knowledge and tools, and the agent reasons through the conversation. It runs inside your CRM, which in practice means HighLevel, HubSpot or a custom stack, and it takes over the text channels that already flow through that CRM. There is a drag-and-drop builder, a testing portal you can use before any real lead sees the agent, and a Smart FAQ feature that flags questions the agent cannot answer confidently instead of inventing one.

SalesAPE is an AI sales assistant for inbound response. Its agents engage leads coming through email, SMS, web chat and WhatsApp, qualify them, answer common questions, and either book a meeting or hand the lead to a human with a summary dropped into the CRM. The company's own framing is that it handles the inbound reply your sales team is too slow or too busy to send. The site says a new agent takes roughly four to five weeks to configure, because the team crawls your website, builds FAQs, trains on your tone of voice and runs simulated conversations before you go live.

One is a platform you operate. The other is a service you supervise.

A few details that matter more than the headline numbers:

- On business plans, message costs sit inside the base price. You are not metering every reply on top of the subscription unless you exceed your ceiling, and overages draw from a wallet.
- A "message" equals one segment, except with the Agent Node's unlimited mode, where billing shifts to token costs and a single reply can consume several segments. Heavy agents cost more than the tier name suggests.
- There are no refunds. You get a free-forever plan under 100 messages and a 7-day trial of any paid plan to decide before you are billed.
- You cannot bring your own OpenAI or Anthropic key to cut costs. CloseBot treats that as a security decision.
- The agency plan is the one people actually talk about, because it turns a software expense into a margin line. You mark up the rebillable per-message cost and keep the difference.

Business pricing scales with volume rather than seat count. Publicly listed tiers start at the entry plan and climb through roughly 1,000, 2,000 and 5,000 monthly replies before jumping to the tens of thousands. As a rough shape of that curve: around $64/mo at the bottom, about $109/mo at 2,000 replies, roughly $176/mo at 5,000, and into the several-hundred-dollar range once you pass 20,000 replies a month. If you are not sure which band you land in, the free plan will tell you within a fortnight.

SalesAPE does not publish pricing at all. Their FAQ answers the cost question with a question: how much are unqualified leads costing you? Third-party listings have surfaced two tiers at around $999/mo for a Pro AI Agent and around $1,499/mo for an Elite AI Agent, with annual billing shaving roughly $1,200 to $3,500 off and a setup fee in the region of $3,000 mentioned by at least one reviewer. Treat all of those as reported figures rather than confirmed list prices, because the only way to get a real number is a demo call, and the only way to compare properly is to ask what the quote includes: setup, platform fee, conversation or lead volume, channel scope, support time, and contract length.

That is not an argument that SalesAPE is overpriced. It is an argument that you cannot calculate your cost per booked appointment until late in the sales process, and if you need to model that before a budget conversation, that opacity is a real cost of its own.

## Setup time: minutes versus weeks

CloseBot's timeline is measured in hours. Build with the drag-and-drop builder, pressure-test it in the testing portal, connect the CRM, go live. The company's own line is "minutes, not weeks," and G2 reviewers back the spirit of it even when they flag a learning curve. One reviewer noted the platform is not something you switch on and expect perfect results from; another said the initial setup takes minutes but that overcomplicated builds work against you.

SalesAPE's timeline is four to five weeks, and the company states that openly. Most of it is spent learning your business, configuring CRM permissions and sending domains, and running simulated conversations so you can try to trip the agent up before it talks to a real lead. There are reports of an expedited launch path under specific conditions, including native HubSpot integration, but four to five weeks is the standard.

Neither is wrong. A managed service that crawls your site and builds your FAQs takes time. A self-serve builder takes your time instead. If you need something live next week, one of these options is a bad fit on day one.

Two asymmetries in that table are worth spelling out.

First, CloseBot is text-only. There is no voice or video agent, and the product is explicit that conversations stay inside your CRM's existing channels. If your leads want to be phoned, you need something else in the stack.

Second, SalesAPE is inbound-only. It is not an outbound prospecting tool and does not include a prospect database or LinkedIn sequences. That is fine when your problem is a queue of enquiries nobody answers, and useless when your problem is an empty pipeline.

Also worth knowing: CloseBot's marketing leans hard on agency economics, claiming 1,000+ agencies on the platform, more than a million booked appointments, and roughly 150,000 messages a day. Those are vendor numbers. They read as consistent with a mature product rather than a weekend launch, but they are not audited, and you should weigh them as marketing.

## What users say

CloseBot's review base is the larger of the two. G2 shows a 4.8 average across 191 reviews. The recurring praise is conversation quality, the granularity of the flow builder, and the speed of setup. The recurring complaints are more interesting: a learning curve when you over-build, reporting that makes it hard to attribute bookings between the bot and the human team, and occasional API disconnects after GoHighLevel updates. One reviewer specifically disliked the pricing being a single flat dollar figure regardless of market, which is a fair point if you are outside the US.

SalesAPE's public review footprint is thinner and less consistent. Trustpilot currently shows a 4-star average from 33 reviews, with recent praise centred on speed to lead, after-hours coverage, hands-on onboarding and HubSpot integration. Third-party write-ups report scores from 3.4/5 to 4.5/5 depending on when they were compiled and how many reviews they counted, alongside complaints about demo scheduling friction, cancellation difficulty, and support responsiveness. The company replies to negative Trustpilot reviews, which at least shows someone is watching.

For a tool asking for four figures a month, that is enough signal to justify a demo and not enough to skip due diligence. Ask for transcript examples from hard conversations: an angry lead, a pricing objection, someone demanding a human, and a bad-fit enquiry. Those edge cases decide whether your agent books revenue or creates cleanup work.

## The cost nobody puts in the comparison table

CloseBot rides on your CRM, so its price is rarely your whole bill. If you are not already running HighLevel or HubSpot, you are adding a CRM subscription on top. A solo operator wanting around 1,000 AI replies a month is realistically looking at something in the region of $150 to $200 a month combined before any channel fees. That is not a knock on the pricing; it is the honest total.

SalesAPE flips the arithmetic. One larger invoice, no CRM required, but you are paying for someone else's implementation work, and you are dependent on their team for changes. If you want a new qualification question live today, that is a ticket, not a toggle.

If your agency resells AI, the maths changes again. The agency plan exists specifically so your clients pay you and you pay the platform, with your markup sitting in between. On a tool you build yourself, the margin is the reason to bother.

## Three questions that settle it

1. Do you already run a CRM? If yes, CloseBot plugs into something you own, and the upgrade path is straightforward. If no, adding a CRM just to host an agent is a real detour, and a standalone service starts to make more sense.
2. Who is building and maintaining the agent? If nobody on your team wants that job, a platform you configure yourself will quietly rot. If you have someone who will test, tune and watch transcripts, the self-serve route pays for itself.
3. Is your problem response speed or pipeline volume? SalesAPE is built to answer people who already contacted you. CloseBot can do that too, but only inside the channels your CRM already handles. Neither generates leads out of thin air.

## Common questions

**Can I try either one without committing?**
CloseBot has a free plan capped at 100 messages a month, plus a 7-day trial of any paid tier. SalesAPE promotes testing its agent for free, but there is no self-serve trial with published terms; you discuss a pilot on the demo call.

**Does CloseBot work with Instagram and WhatsApp DMs?**
Only through your CRM. It has no standalone Instagram or WhatsApp connection, so the channel has to be wired into HighLevel, HubSpot or a custom CRM first.

**Can SalesAPE handle voice calls?**
Voice is not presented as a core public capability on their site, which leads with email, SMS, chat and WhatsApp. Confirm current voice support directly rather than assuming it.

**Which one is cheaper?**
At low volume, CloseBot clearly is, and the free tier means the first month costs nothing at all. Once you are past several thousand replies a month and need HIPAA compliance and SLAs, the comparison narrows quickly against a quote-based, managed service.

**Will either of them close deals for me?**
No. Both qualify leads and put meetings on the calendar. The close still happens on the call with a human, and any vendor implying otherwise is overselling.

## The bottom line

CloseBot wins on transparency, speed and agency economics. Published plans, a genuinely usable free tier, HIPAA on the upper tier, and a re-billing model built for people who sell AI as a service. Its weakness is that it assumes you will do the work, and it is text-only.

SalesAPE wins if you want the work off your plate and your problem is specifically slow inbound response. It costs more, it takes a month to launch, and you will not see a price until you talk to someone. Whether that is a dealbreaker depends on whether you are buying software or buying an outcome.
